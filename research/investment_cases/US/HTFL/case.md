## Founder-level executive summary

Heartflow is a strong and improving business, but the Q2 2026 event is a case study in why **strong earnings + a large price gap do not automatically equal New Ignition**. The scanner correctly identified HTFL as deserving immediate P0 research attention; it overreached when it converted that priority signal into a lifecycle label.

The business evidence is genuinely strong. Q2 2026 revenue was $64.1M, +48% YoY; revenue cases were 84,491, +74%; GAAP gross margin rose to about 83%; management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $246-250M and Plaque revenue guidance to $29-31M. Plaque revenue reached $7.8M in Q2 and FFRCT remained durable. The important economic change is therefore not a first ignition of Heartflow as a company, but a **second-product monetization S-curve inside an already-established FFRCT workflow**.

The stock, however, must be judged from the current price. On 2026-08-14 HTFL closed at $42.08 after a 35.7% one-day gap from $31.01. Using 86.954M basic shares as of 2026-07-31, Q2 options/RSUs/ESPP and a treasury-stock convention, this case estimates about 96.24M economic diluted shares, about $4.05B diluted equity value, and about $3.80B enterprise value excluding operating leases. Against the midpoint of FY2026 revenue guidance ($248M), that is approximately **15.3x EV/FY2026 Sales**. This is not a low-expectation discovery valuation.

The dominant variable is now: **Can Plaque turn Heartflow from a mostly FFRCT case-volume business into a multi-product monetization platform per CCTA fast enough to outrun already-high price-implied expectations, while preserving pricing, gross-margin leverage and per-share economics?**

The durable case lesson is a stage-classification rule: `P0 research priority != New Ignition lifecycle`. A New Ignition label must survive a current diluted-EV and reverse-return test, must respect the company's prior lifecycle, and must distinguish procedure/patient volume from revenue cases and product attach. HTFL fails the strict first-ignition test after the Q2 rerating but remains a high-quality Active Case in **Confirmation / Early Momentum with second-S-curve optionality**.

### Reverse 3x / 5x / 10x math

The purpose is not to predict a target price; it is to test whether the **current price still has early-stage mathematical room**. Illustratively assume 105M diluted shares by 2030, $150M net cash, and 8x/10x/12x terminal EV/Sales.

At a 10x terminal sales multiple:

- **3x stock outcome** from ~$42 requires roughly **$1.31B 2030 revenue**, about **52% CAGR** from the $248M FY2026 midpoint.
- **5x stock outcome** requires roughly **$2.19B 2030 revenue**, about **72% CAGR**.
- **10x stock outcome** requires roughly **$4.40B 2030 revenue**, about **105% CAGR**.

Even a 12x terminal multiple still requires unusually high sustained growth for a 3x-5x result over this four-year window. A 7-10 year horizon can restore mathematical feasibility if Heartflow becomes a category platform and the asymptomatic market opens, but that is a different lifecycle claim from "new ignition today."

### Dominant variable

The current dominant variable is **Plaque monetization per CCTA**: can Plaque expand from early attach to broad utilization, including beyond FFRCT-overlap patients, while sustaining pricing and high incremental gross margin?

### Most easily misunderstood link

**Revenue cases.** A revenue case is a delivered analysis, not a unique CCTA, procedure or patient. Ordering FFRCT and Plaque on the same CCTA creates two revenue cases. Therefore `revenue cases +74%` is not equivalent to `procedure volume +74%` or `patient volume +74%`.

### What most directly drove the August 2026 stock move

The Q2 event combined:

1. fundamental estimate revision — total FY2026 guide midpoint +7.8% from Q1 guidance;
2. much larger Plaque estimate revision — Plaque FY guide midpoint about +50% from the prior $20M midpoint;
3. improved margin expectations;
4. higher probability assigned to a multi-product platform and future TAM.

The one-day stock move of +35.7% was therefore too large to explain as near-term revenue revision alone. Under a simple constant-multiple bridge, 1.357 / 1.078 ~= 1.259, implying roughly a **26% additional valuation/probability factor** after the revenue-guide revision. This is an analytical attribution, not proof of the market's exact causal decomposition.

Capital structure did not cause the gap; debt had already been extinguished. Market/sector beta and flow may have influenced the path, but no precise residual is claimed without a dedicated event regression.

## 7. Counterfactual TRQuant discipline

### What would block an erroneous large entry?

A mandatory **current diluted EV + reverse-return gate** after any >20% event gap. At ~$42, 15.3x FY2026 EV/Sales and the 2030 reverse math should block automatic promotion from "strong catalyst" to "large New Ignition buy."

### What would allow a small trial position?

Verified FFRCT durability + Plaque acceleration + gross-margin expansion + sufficient liquidity, with a bounded position and explicit thesis-kill criteria.

### What would trigger an add?

Either:

1. price resets enough to improve expected return while thesis remains intact; or
2. new evidence moves the 2028-2030 revenue/cash-flow path materially above the current Bull Case — especially Plaque attach/utilization, pricing and sustained margin leverage.

### What would trigger a trim?

A large rerating that moves expected return below the portfolio hurdle without a matching estimate revision; or evidence that a momentum position has become extended relative to its original setup.

### What would trigger exit?

Thesis-kill evidence in utilization, pricing, reimbursement, competition, legal status, cash conversion or per-share dilution — not merely ordinary volatility.

## 8. Generalizable lessons

### A. Case-specific observation

HTFL's Q2 2026 was a real acceleration event: revenue +48%, Plaque $7.8M, guidance raised, gross margin near 83%, and the stock gapped +35.7%.

### B. Candidate reusable lesson

**New Ignition must be earned after, not before, the current-price expectation test.** For post-IPO high-growth names, require current economic diluted EV, a metric-ontology check and reverse 3x/5x/10x math before final lifecycle promotion.

A second reusable lesson is that a **new product S-curve is not automatically the company's first ignition**. A mature core product plus a new attach product should often be labeled `second_s_curve_confirmation` or `re_ignition` depending on prior lifecycle evidence.

### C. Counterexample / limitation

A high forward sales multiple does not by itself invalidate a multibagger. If growth, margins and TAM expand far faster than currently modeled, a high-multiple company can still compound dramatically. The rule is to expose required assumptions, not to impose a universal valuation cutoff.

Likewise, a large earnings gap can occur very early in a genuine new winner. The gap is not disqualifying; it simply forces re-underwriting at the new price.

### D. Promotion evidence needed

Promote these lessons beyond `candidate` only after cross-case testing against:

- genuine early winners where a large first gap still left substantial diluted-EV room;
- false-positive high-growth IPOs that later compressed;
- second-S-curve cases such as established platform companies launching a major new product;
- PIT matched controls by size, sector, growth and valuation.

## 9. Golden-case comparison

### HTFL vs CRWV

**Similarity:** both had genuine fundamental acceleration and a large rerating; both show that business quality and stock attractiveness must be separated. CRWV's persisted Golden Case explicitly warns that a correct business thesis can coexist with a poor entry after valuation expansion.

**Difference:** CRWV's dominant risk is capital conversion through debt, leases, interest and capex. HTFL is asset-light and net-cash; its dominant risks are product attach/utilization, reimbursement, competition, legal overhang and dilution. HTFL therefore must not inherit CRWV's capital-intensity ontology mechanically.

**Shared lesson:** strong growth plus price confirmation is not enough; re-underwrite the new price.

### HTFL vs TSM

**Similarity:** both illustrate that a widely recognized structural winner can still produce further upside, but "market knows the story" is different from "price fully reflects future value." Both require expectations analysis rather than narrative novelty.

**Difference:** TSM is a proven profitable compounder with flat dilution and capex productivity as a core variable. HTFL is a recent IPO, loss-making, high-dilution-risk growth company with a second-product S-curve. TSM's lifecycle was already Confirmation/Momentum when first recovered; HTFL similarly should not be forced back to Discovery merely because Q2 was spectacular.

### HTFL vs SNDK methodological anchor

SNDK's reusable lifecycle lesson is `fundamental ignition -> expansion -> extreme expectations -> reset -> re-underwrite -> re-ignition`. HTFL adds a complementary lesson: **second-product ignition inside an existing company must be separated from first-company ignition**, and current diluted-EV math determines whether the new episode still has true right-tail room.
