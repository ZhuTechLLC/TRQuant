## Executive conclusion

Heartflow is **not primarily a macro-driven operating business**, but HTFL is a **macro-sensitive equity because current shareholder value is dominated by long-duration growth expectations and a high forward-sales multiple**.

The macro distinction is therefore:

- **Operating-fundamental relevance:** low to moderate.
- **Valuation / expected-return relevance:** high.
- **Balance-sheet financing sensitivity:** low in the near term because Heartflow has substantial cash/investments and no normal funded debt.
- **Primary external company-specific variable:** reimbursement / coverage / clinical adoption, which is policy and industry structure rather than generic macro.

The current macro state should not reverse the constructive business thesis. It should, however, make entry price and position sizing more demanding after the Q2 2026 rerating.

## Macro state as of 2026-08-18

### Monetary policy and real rates

The Federal Reserve maintained the federal-funds target range at **3.50%-3.75%** on 2026-07-29. The statement characterized inflation as still elevated relative to the 2% goal and explicitly cited supply shocks, including energy, while three FOMC voters preferred a 25 bp hike.

On 2026-08-18 the U.S. Treasury curve showed approximately:

- 2-year nominal Treasury: **4.19%**
- 10-year nominal Treasury: **4.71%**
- 10-year real Treasury: **2.41%**

This is a high real-discount-rate environment for an unprofitable growth equity whose value is weighted toward future revenue, margin and free-cash-flow realization.

### Inflation

July 2026 CPI was **+3.4% YoY** and core CPI **+2.5% YoY**. Energy CPI was **+14.7% YoY**. Core disinflation is constructive, but energy and geopolitical supply shocks leave a path by which nominal and real yields remain elevated.

For Heartflow, this is primarily a valuation channel. It can also affect provider budgets indirectly, although this case does not claim a measurable direct revenue elasticity to CPI.

### Growth and labor

Q2 2026 real GDP grew at a **1.5% annualized rate**, while real final sales to private domestic purchasers grew **3.9%**, indicating slower headline growth but firmer underlying private demand.

July payroll employment changed by **-23,000** and unemployment was **4.1%**. Importantly for Heartflow's end-market context, health-care employment still increased **22,000**, including **18,000** in ambulatory health-care services.

The evidence therefore does not support a current macro thesis of collapsing health-care utilization. Broad labor conditions are softer, but the health-care sector remains comparatively resilient.

## Company-specific transmission map

### 1. Real yield / discount-rate channel — HIGH importance

HTFL's reconstructed post-Q2 valuation is about **15.3x FY2026 EV/Sales** at the 2026-08-14 checkpoint. The company remains GAAP loss-making and is targeting future cash-flow profitability rather than producing large current shareholder cash flow.

Therefore the equity behaves as a long-duration growth asset: a higher real discount rate can reduce the multiple investors are willing to pay even if the operating thesis remains intact.

This is the most important macro channel.

### 2. Risk-premium / growth-equity appetite — HIGH importance

After a +35.7% one-day earnings gap, a meaningful share of future Plaque/platform optionality was capitalized into price. A later risk-off shock can compress the multiple independently of company execution.

This review deliberately does not persist licensed raw credit-spread index observations into the repository. Cross-asset indicators may be used as contextual checks, while the durable case stores public-domain macro evidence and derived interpretation.

### 3. Balance-sheet / financing channel — LOW near-term importance

At 2026-06-30 Heartflow reported about **$246.84M** of cash and investments and no normal funded financial debt. Consequently, higher policy rates do not create a CRWV-like debt-service problem.

High rates can still make future external capital more expensive if cash burn persists, but current macro risk is primarily a valuation issue rather than an immediate solvency or interest-expense issue.

### 4. Health-care utilization / recession channel — LOW-TO-MODERATE importance

Heartflow revenue depends on clinical CCTA workflows rather than discretionary consumer purchases. A severe recession could reduce elective diagnostics, commercial insurance coverage or provider budgets, but July 2026 labor evidence still showed positive health-care and ambulatory employment growth.

The current evidence therefore supports a **defensive-to-neutral operating macro exposure**, not a cyclical-collapse thesis.

### 5. Provider cost inflation / productivity channel — MIXED, LOW-TO-MODERATE importance

Inflation can pressure hospital and physician budgets and slow procurement decisions. Conversely, a cloud-based AI workflow that reduces manual interpretation or improves care-path efficiency can become more valuable when labor costs are high.

The second effect is a plausible inference, not a reported HTFL elasticity, and should not be treated as a quantified bull factor without provider-level evidence.

### 6. FX / tariffs / commodity channel — LOW importance

Heartflow is a software/clinical-analysis platform rather than a hardware-heavy manufacturer. The case therefore does not assign material first-order exposure to commodity prices or tariffs. Geopolitical energy shocks matter mainly through inflation, rates and market risk appetite.

## Interaction with the false-New-Ignition diagnosis

The macro review strengthens the prior stage correction.

A stock at roughly 15x forward sales in a ~2.4% 10-year real-yield environment is not automatically uninvestable, but it carries meaningful duration risk. Therefore a scanner should not interpret a strong earnings gap as "more New Ignition" without re-underwriting:

`new fundamentals -> new price -> current diluted EV -> macro discount-rate context -> reverse return math -> lifecycle classification`

This is especially important for unprofitable post-IPO growth equities.

## Current decision implication

**Business thesis:** unchanged / constructive.  
**Macro operating bias:** neutral.  
**Macro valuation bias:** mildly negative at the current high multiple because real yields remain high.  
**Risk-budget implication:** require a larger expected-return margin of safety; do not increase position size merely because Q2 improved thesis confidence.

The dominant variable remains **Plaque monetization per CCTA and reimbursement-enabled adoption**, not the federal-funds rate. Macro is an overlay on the price paid for that thesis.
