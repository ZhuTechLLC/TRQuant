## Executive conclusion

TSMC remains one of the highest-quality direct beneficiaries of the AI/HPC infrastructure cycle. The operating thesis is **strong and validated**: Q2 2026 revenue was $40.20B, gross margin 67.7%, operating margin 60.3%, and Q3 revenue guidance is $44.6-$45.8B. The dominant investment question is no longer whether AI demand exists. It is whether TSMC can continue converting leading-edge and advanced-packaging scarcity into earnings and free cash flow fast enough to outrun an already-high consensus while preserving capital productivity and avoiding policy/geopolitical impairment.

The macro overlay is deliberately split. **Operating demand is Green** because major hyperscalers continue to commit exceptional resources to AI compute, Microsoft reports capacity constraints and a high GPU/CPU share of current CapEx, Meta maintains a $130-$145B 2026 CapEx range including finance-lease principal, and NVIDIA's latest reported Data Center revenue was $75.2B, +92% YoY. **Discount-rate and risk-budget conditions are Yellow-Red** because the Fed target range remains 3.50%-3.75% and June core PCE was 3.3% YoY. **Taiwan geopolitical operational risk remains a Red Tail**: low-frequency but severe, and it must be treated as an explicit kill/re-underwrite pathway rather than diluted into a normal valuation multiple.

TSM closed 2026-08-18 at **$413.41**, down from $430.97 the prior session and approximately 13.7% below the 52-week high of $478.89. This enters the case's **$395-$420 First Accumulation / Buy Review zone** and independently satisfies the existing >=12% drawdown exception trigger. The correct response is **START SMALL / BUY REVIEW, conditional**, not automatic buying. The current price reset is useful only if AI/HPC demand, EPS/guidance, N2/advanced-packaging execution and operational geopolitics remain intact.

## 1. Historical point-in-time reconstruction

The earliest recovered user-side explicit TSM research record is 2025-08-19. By that date, the broad AI thesis was not hidden: TSMC had already disclosed AI accelerator revenue expected to double in 2025, CoWoS capacity expected to double, N2 volume production in H2 2025, and an internal planning view approaching mid-40s percentage AI-accelerator revenue CAGR for five years from 2024. Major hyperscalers had also disclosed large and rising infrastructure programs.

Therefore the missed opportunity was **not** 'discover AI before everyone else.' The more precise missed edge was:

> The market recognized AI, but continued to underestimate the magnitude and duration of TSMC's earnings conversion.

The recovered sell-side FY2026 ADR EPS consensus available before 2025-08-19 was about $10.95. By the current review it is about $16.45, an increase of roughly 50%. This is the central expectation-revision fact in the case.

No contemporaneous TSM buy/add/trim/stop/target record has been recovered. Linked IBKR history showed no TSM trades in the checked 2025 Q3/Q4 windows, but that does not prove there were no trades at another broker. Historical action remains a retrospective process reconstruction, not a fabricated ex-ante recommendation.

## 2. Business quality and economic engine

TSMC's value-driver chain is:

`AI/HPC end demand -> customer designs/tapeouts -> leading-edge + advanced-packaging demand -> installed capacity -> utilization/mix/pricing -> revenue -> margins -> operating cash flow -> CapEx -> free cash flow -> shareholder value`.

The key distinction is that high CapEx is not automatically bad for TSMC. The question is **incremental capital productivity**. In 2026 Q2, the company reported extremely high margins even while investing aggressively. The thesis remains strong as long as advanced capacity earns attractive economics and demand keeps utilization high.

Important company evidence:
- Q2 2026 revenue: $40.20B.
- Q2 gross margin: 67.7%.
- Q2 operating margin: 60.3%.
- Q3 revenue guide: $44.6-$45.8B.
- Q3 gross-margin guide: 65%-67%.
- Q3 operating-margin guide: 56%-58%.
- <=7nm share of Q2 wafer revenue: 77%; N2 began contributing 3%.

The company is therefore not an early-loss 'AI option.' It is a proven, cash-generative bottleneck with very high market expectations.

## 3. Macro & Cross-Asset Context

### 3.1 Rates / inflation — Yellow-Red valuation overlay

The Fed maintained a 3.50%-3.75% target range at the July 29, 2026 meeting. June core PCE inflation was 3.3% YoY. These conditions raise the required return for long-duration growth equities and can compress valuation even when operating data remain strong.

For TSMC, this is principally a **capital-cost / valuation channel** rather than evidence of wafer-demand weakness. A price decline caused mainly by higher discount rates can improve expected return if EPS expectations and operating evidence remain intact.

### 3.2 Growth — Yellow-Green, secondary to AI demand

Q2 2026 US real GDP grew at a 1.5% annualized rate in the advance estimate, slower than Q1's 2.1%. For TSMC, aggregate GDP is relevant but less decision-useful than customer-level cloud/AI spending, smartphone/auto cycles and semiconductor design activity.

### 3.3 Hyperscaler AI infrastructure — Green

The correct upstream signal is not headline CapEx alone. Decompose:
- GPU/CPU/accelerator/server spend;
- data-center buildings and networking;
- finance leases;
- component-price inflation;
- merchant GPU vs custom ASIC;
- cloud/AI revenue, backlog and monetization;
- capacity constraints.

Current examples demonstrate why this matters. Microsoft reported Q3 FY26 CapEx of $31.9B and said roughly two-thirds was short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs; it expects next-quarter CapEx above $40B and roughly $190B of calendar-2026 CapEx, including about $25B from higher component pricing. Meta guides 2026 CapEx including finance-lease principal to $130-$145B. NVIDIA's Q1 FY27 Data Center revenue was $75.2B, +92% YoY, with Q2 total revenue guided to $91B +/-2% excluding China Data Center compute.

These data support a continued strong real-compute cycle, but they also show why a nominal CapEx chart can mislead: some spending growth is price inflation, construction or leases rather than incremental semiconductor units.

### 3.4 Semiconductor cycle — Green / high expectations

TSMC's reported revenue, margin and guidance currently confirm the operating cycle. The risk has shifted from 'is demand real?' toward:
- how much of future demand is already embedded in estimates;
- whether N2/A16 and advanced packaging execute on time;
- whether custom ASIC and GPU architectures remain favorable to TSMC;
- whether high CapEx continues to produce high utilization and FCF.

### 3.5 USD/TWD — Yellow monitor

FX affects reported TWD economics, margins and ADR/local-share parity. It also matters for historical EV reconstruction. Production-grade attribution should use official same-date FX rather than a rough quarter-average convention. This remains one reason the case is not promoted to decision_grade.

### 3.6 Export controls / industrial policy — Yellow structural risk

Export controls can change customer shipment mix, China market access and accelerator demand. Geographic manufacturing diversification can reduce concentration risk while raising overseas-fab cost. These are two-sided transmission channels; neither 'export restriction bad' nor 'subsidy good' is sufficient without a company-specific earnings bridge.

### 3.7 Taiwan geopolitics — Red Tail

The correct treatment is event-conditioned, not a permanent generic discount only. A verified event that materially impairs production, logistics, personnel mobility, export capability or market access is an immediate re-underwrite and possible thesis kill. Headlines without operating evidence should not create automatic trades.

## 4. Expectations and valuation

### Historical bridge

At 2025-08-19:
- price $232.70;
- FY2026 sell-side ADR EPS consensus about $10.95;
- forward P/E about 21.25x.

At 2026-08-18 close:
- price $413.41;
- FY2026 sell-side ADR EPS consensus about $16.45;
- forward P/E about 25.1x;
- FY2027 consensus about $20.83, implying about 19.8x FY2027 EPS.

From 2025-08-19 to 2026-08-18, the price increased about 77.7%, while the recovered FY2026 EPS consensus increased about 50.2% and the forward multiple expanded by a smaller amount. The dominant fundamental driver was therefore earnings revision, with multiple expansion secondary.

This is the most reusable lesson from the case: **a widely known narrative can still contain a large expectation gap when the market repeatedly underestimates earnings magnitude/duration.**

### Current valuation judgment

TSM is no longer cheap in an absolute sense, but the current price is materially more attractive than at the recent high if the EPS path remains intact. The right question is not 'is 25x forward P/E expensive?' but:

`What growth/margin/capital-productivity path is embedded in 25x, and what evidence could move the denominator faster than the price?`

At $413.41, the case has moved from unconditional 'watch for a better entry' into **active buy-review territory**. It is not yet a full-size conviction instruction because consensus remains high, macro discount rates are restrictive and geopolitical tail risk cannot be diversified away inside the single name.

## 7. Add / hold / trim / avoid logic

### Add
- company guidance/EPS revisions rise enough that expected return improves despite price;
- upstream real-compute evidence strengthens and the price has not fully repriced it;
- price/valuation compresses materially while AI/HPC demand, technology execution, margins and operations remain intact.

### Hold
- thesis intact, estimates progressing, valuation supportable, but no large new expectation gap.

### Trim
- price/multiple expands much faster than EPS estimates;
- portfolio AI/semiconductor correlation becomes excessive;
- expected return compresses without new fundamental evidence.

### Avoid / exit / thesis re-underwrite
- multiple quarters of AI/HPC demand deterioration plus major customer real-compute cuts;
- structural N2/A16/advanced-packaging failure or meaningful design migration;
- persistent margin impairment beyond understandable overseas-ramp effects;
- elevated CapEx with deteriorating utilization, revenue guidance and FCF;
- verified geopolitical operational impairment.
