# Micron Technology MU — Investment Case

**As of:** 2026-08-23  
**Status:** `research_complete`  
**Current action:** `START-SMALL / BUY REVIEW — CONDITIONAL`, no order created.

## Decision question
Has AI memory demand, HBM technology leadership and multi-year take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements structurally improved Micron's cycle durability enough that the market is still pricing an excessive reversion from current earnings, or are record margins simply the peak of an unusually powerful memory cycle?

## What is established
- FY26 Q3 revenue was $41.456bn versus $23.860bn QoQ and $9.301bn YoY.
- Q3 GAAP gross margin was 84.6%; GAAP net income $28.243bn / $24.67 diluted EPS; operating cash flow $25.388bn and adjusted FCF $18.304bn.
- FY26 Q4 guidance: revenue $50.0bn ±$1.0bn, gross margin ~86%, non-GAAP diluted EPS $31.00 ±$1.00.
- HBM4 is in high-volume shipments for the lead customer's platform and has samples at multiple end-customers; HBM4E volume production is expected in calendar 2027.
- Micron disclosed 16 Strategic Customer Agreements that represent roughly 20% of DRAM volume and one-third of NAND volume over the agreement period. Typical term is five years (2026-2030), they are take-or-pay, and management described approximately $100bn of contracts plus ~$22bn of deposits/related commitments under agreements signed so far.
- Direct IBKR 2026-08-21 close was $966.78, versus a 52-week high around $1,255.

## Thesis
The unusual opportunity is not simply 'HBM demand is strong'. The more important structural question is whether take-or-pay SCAs, constrained supply growth and HBM content make Micron's earnings **less mean-reverting than historical memory-cycle heuristics assume**. If Q4 guidance is close to a sustainable earnings level, current price may already discount a large fall in earnings.

## Counter-evidence
- Q3/Q4 margins near the mid-80s are extraordinary for commodity/semi memory and should not be capitalized as permanent without evidence.
- Large capex and capacity additions can eventually create oversupply.
- HBM/customer concentration and technology transitions can shift rapidly.
- Current share price has already risen dramatically over the prior year; the case is now about durability, not discovery of AI memory.
- Strategic Customer Agreements improve visibility but do not eliminate pricing, technology, execution or industry-cycle risk.

## Reverse valuation
At the 2026-08-21 close of $966.78, a 15% annualized two-year return implies about $1,278.57 in 2028. Required 2028 EPS:
- ~$106.55 at 12x P/E;
- ~$127.86 at 10x;
- ~$159.82 at 8x.

Management's Q4 FY26 non-GAAP EPS guide of $31 per quarter annualizes mechanically to ~$124, **not a forecast**. Thus a 10x 2028 exit multiple requires earnings roughly around the Q4 guided run-rate; the stock is highly attractive only if the new memory regime proves durable enough to avoid a major earnings collapse.

## Bull / Base / Bear
- **Bull:** HBM/AI memory shortage persists, SCAs improve price/volume visibility, HBM4/HBM4E share expands, and normalized EPS remains near or above the reverse hurdle.
- **Base:** margins normalize from exceptional levels but remain structurally above old-cycle averages; returns depend on how quickly the market revises normalized earnings.
- **Bear:** capacity/supply catches up, ASP/margins mean-revert sharply, HBM share/technology slips, or capex absorbs cash as the cycle turns.

## Kill criteria
1. DRAM/HBM pricing and customer commitments weaken materially across multiple quarters.
2. Q4/FY27 margins fall much faster than supply/demand evidence would justify.
3. HBM4/HBM4E loses qualification/share at major AI platforms.
4. Capex rises while FCF and return on invested capital deteriorate, signaling oversupply.
5. SCA economics or enforceability prove weaker than disclosed take-or-pay structure suggests.
